Following the Bybit heist: three laundering branches, traced hop by hop

The $1.4–1.5 billion theft from Bybit on 21 February 2025 is the largest crypto heist on record. This report follows three representative branches of the stolen ETH from the exploiter's wallet to their exits, checking each hop against the raw transaction record.

Bottom line

  • Within 14 minutes of the theft, the exploiter funded a second wallet. Within 27 minutes, that wallet was turning stolen Lido stETH into ETH through DEX aggregators. In 71 minutes it received 98,047.84 ETH in swap proceeds, which roughly matches the ~98,375 stETH and mETH stolen.
  • Branch A moved 257.27 ETH from the exploiter to THORChain in 16 minutes through two single-use wallets. The THORChain deposit memo shows a swap to a Bitcoin address.
  • Branch C split funds from a labeled exploiter wallet, then merged 2,083 ETH from six wallets into one address. It sent a 1 ETH test, moved the rest to a holding wallet, and left it untouched for 70 days before moving it again in May 2025.
  • Third parties ran address poisoning against the launderers. About 98% of the exploiter's 4,418 transactions are incoming dust from look-alike addresses. That noise can mislead analysts who match addresses by their first and last characters.
  • I assess with high confidence that the funds in all three branches came from the Bybit theft. Each hop below links to a transaction anyone can check.

1Background

Bybit's Ethereum cold wallet (0x1Db92e2EeBC8E0c075a02BeA49a2935BcD2dFCF4) was a Safe multisig. Attackers compromised a Safe{Wallet} developer environment and injected JavaScript into the Safe web app that activated only for Bybit's wallet. When signers approved what looked like a routine transfer at 14:13:35 UTC, they signed a contract upgrade that handed control to the attacker.

Ninety-six seconds later, a sweepETH call moved 401,346.77 ETH to 0x47666Fab8bd0Ac7003bce3f5C3585383F09486E2, labeled "Bybit Exploiter 1" on Etherscan. Public reporting puts the total haul at about 401,000 ETH, 90,375 stETH, 15,000 cmETH and 8,000 mETH. mETH Protocol later recovered the 15,000 cmETH. On 26 February 2025 the FBI attributed the theft to North Korea ("TraderTraitor") and published 51 Ethereum addresses for the industry to block.

3Scope, method and confidence

The theft fanned out to thousands of addresses, so tracing every unit is a job for a team with commercial tooling. I chose three branches, one for each phase of the laundering: liquidation (turning staked ETH into plain ETH), conversion (moving ETH off Ethereum) and layering (splitting and merging to break the trail).

I checked every hop against the transaction record on Etherscan: sender, receiver, amount, block and timestamp. I linked hops through exact amount matches, timing and Etherscan's "funded by" field. I decoded the THORChain deposit memo from the transaction's input data. Figures from news and vendor reports are marked Reported and kept apart from my own findings.

5Findings

Liquid staking tokens were dumped first, through aggregators

Exploiter 2 got 1 ETH of gas 13 minutes after the theft, approved ParaSwap's Delta V2 contract to spend stETH, and collected 98,047.84 ETH in 12 swaps over 44 minutes. That total is within 0.4% of the 98,375 stETH and mETH reported stolen. Staked ETH can be frozen or blocklisted by its issuer, so it was the riskiest asset to hold. Converting it first and fast follows from that. Assessment: high confidence these swaps liquidated the stolen stETH and mETH. I did not view the token transfer that moved stETH into Exploiter 2, so that link rests on the approval, the amounts and the timing.

Single-use wallets fed THORChain within minutes

In branch A, each of two fresh wallets received exactly 257.26 ETH and passed it on within 14 minutes and 2 minutes. Each kept only dust. The final deposit's memo, =:b:191du9ivk3k5YekuXFgtkoVei6pK76wZmf:0/1/0:dx:10, asks THORChain to swap the ETH to native BTC, deliver it to that address and run it as a streaming swap. The memo also carries an affiliate tag. Public reporting attributes about 72% of the converted ETH to THORChain, and branch A shows that route at the level of a single transaction.

Split, merge, test, then wait

Exploiter 5 sent ETH to three wallets that each forwarded the exact amount to one merge wallet within 20 minutes. Three more wallets, not traced here, added 1,100 ETH, for 2,083.00 ETH in total. The merge wallet sent a 1 ETH test to a new address, then the remaining 2,082.07 ETH an hour later. That address then sat untouched for 69 days and 23 hours. It reappeared in May 2025 with transfers of 500.1 ETH and 513.78 ETH. Assessment: the dormancy points to the launderers waiting for attention to fade. 983.00 ETH (47%) of the merged funds trace directly to Exploiter 5. The rest arrived at the same time and in the same pattern but is unverified.

The launderers were themselves being poisoned

Exploiter 1 has 4,418 transactions, but its nonce shows it sent only about 62. Roughly 4,350 (about 98%) are incoming dust or zero-value transfers. Many come from addresses that copy the first and last characters of wallets the launderers had just used, hoping someone copies the wrong address from their history. The same pattern appears around the holding wallet in May. For investigators, this noise inflates transaction counts and can mislead any tool or analyst that matches addresses by prefix and suffix. Several zero-value transfers also appear to come from an address labeled "FixedFloat Dep". Zero-value token transfers can be spoofed, so I treat those as noise, not as evidence of an exchange deposit.

6The wider picture Reported

  • About 401,000 ETH plus ~113,000 in staked ETH derivatives were stolen, worth $1.4–1.5 billion at the time.
  • On the first day, the haul was split into roughly 40–50 wallets. Within a week the number of addresses reached the thousands, and the FBI's notice lists 51 key Ethereum addresses.
  • Arkham and others report that about 72% of the converted ETH crossed to Bitcoin through THORChain. eXch, a no-KYC swap service, handled about 79,655 ETH (about 16%).
  • On 4 March 2025, Bybit's CEO said 77% of the funds were still traceable, 20% had "gone dark" and 3% had been frozen. Tether and Circle froze stablecoins linked to the hack, and Chainflip refused deposits from flagged addresses.

7Open leads

  1. Look up THORChain's outbound transaction for inbound hash 0x16ed29f9…fa1a, then follow BTC address 191du9ivk3k5YekuXFgtkoVei6pK76wZmf on Bitcoin. Watch for consolidation, mixing or exchange deposits.
  2. Follow the May 2025 outflows from the holding wallet (500.1 ETH and 513.78 ETH) and the rest of its 138 transactions.
  3. Trace back the three wallets that put 1,100 ETH into the merge wallet, to confirm they come from the same theft.
  4. Pull the stETH and mETH token transfer history of Exploiter 2 to close the one inferred link in branch B.
  5. Screen every address in this report against the FBI's list and commercial attribution data.

8Recommendations for compliance teams

  • Screen for indirect exposure as well as direct. Every wallet in branches A and C is unlabeled, one to three hops from a listed exploiter address. Screening only for direct contact misses them.
  • Treat dormant wallets as live risk. Branch C funds stayed still for 70 days. Alert on sudden activity from wallets that are a few hops from a known hack.
  • Hold BTC deposits from cross-chain swaps for review when they arrive in the weeks after a major hack, especially from THORChain streaming swaps.
  • Compare full addresses, never just the prefix and suffix, in investigations, withdrawal allowlists and case notes. Address poisoning is aimed at exactly that shortcut.
